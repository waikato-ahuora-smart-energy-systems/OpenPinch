# HEN Retrofit

## Overview
Retrofit of an existing heat exchanger network: a search, derived from the network evolution method, that adds one modification at a time (a new match, added area on an existing unit, or, optionally, an existing recovery unit relocated, and a new or moved match paired with the new end heater or cooler it needs) to the existing network and evaluates each candidate with the fixed-structure model's "retrofit" goal (`../models/fixed_structure.py`). The best design at each depth makes a frontier over the number of steps (structural changes, by default). `search.py` runs the search, `moves.py` holds the structural modifications and the screen that ranks new matches, `heat_pump_streams.py` and `heat_pump_targets.py` the heat pump a search starts from as streams and its as-piped target, `loops.py` the heat recovery loops that link processes on a site (and the constant-temperature utilities they add), and `frontier.py` marks dominated designs and `processes.py` maps each stream to its process zone. Results use the contracts in `OpenPinch/contracts/synthesis/retrofit.py`.

## Interface
- `RetrofitSettings`, `run_retrofit_search(problem, existing, settings, *, heat_pump=None)` (`search.py`): search limits and retrofit fixed costs, and the search, returning a `HeatExchangerNetworkRetrofitResult`.
- `HeatPumpStep(record, condensers, evaporators, base, ambient_sources=())` (`heat_pump_streams.py`): a heat pump of a fixed size a search starts from. The search's `problem` holds the machine's `condensers` and `evaporators`, and the hot streams of the air its evaporators draw on (`ambient_sources`), as process streams; each ambient source is matched to every evaporator that stands `ambient_approach` below it at both ends of a counter-current match (within 1e-6 K), in a new stage after the last; the search uses its `approach`, or with none `HPR_DT_ENV_CONT` + `HPR_DT_CONT`, and the refusal quotes the approach a stream was held to. A stream of the step that nothing can serve at the approach raises `ValueError` naming it; `record` is the `NetworkHeatPump` with its electricity ($/y) and compressor costs; `base` is the existing network's design from the search without the heat pump. With `heat_pump=step` the search's first design (`frontier[0]`) is the existing network with the matches `with_heat_pump_matches` gives (placed from `base`'s solved coolers and heaters, at `settings.approach`, or with none at each process stream's own contribution, with the zone's dt_cont multiplier, plus `HPR_DT_CONT`, as the evaluator's `hot_contributions` and `cold_contributions` give it by existing name, each side apart since a zone may name a hot and a cold stream alike; a refusal then quotes "each stream's contribution plus" `HPR_DT_CONT`), each a `new_match` modification priced as any new match, after one `heat_pump` modification that names the machine and holds the compressor's cost, COP and condenser capacity. Every design's `utility_cost` includes the machine's electricity, its savings are against `base.utility_cost`, and its network records the machine in `heat_pumps`. The step counts as one of `max_modifications`; later steps screen new matches and new utility units on the process's own streams only. A step whose first design does not solve raises `ValueError`.
- `NewMatch` (`moves.py`): a new recovery match, hot and cold stream names and a 1-based stage, joining that stage or (`new_stage`) in a stage inserted there.
- `apply_new_match(network, move, exchanger_id)` (`moves.py`): the network with the match added.
- `with_heat_pump_matches(network, *, condensers, evaporators, approach, machine="HP", solved=None, ambient_sources=(), ambient_approach=None, hot_approaches=None, cold_approaches=None)` (`heat_pump_streams.py`; `hot_approaches` and `cold_approaches` map a hot or a cold process stream to its own approach in place of `approach`): a network (solved, or with `solved=` its solved form) with the matches that serve a heat pump's streams, and a `PlacedMatch` for each exchanger added: its id (`"new:<machine>:<hot>-><cold>"`, with `:2`, `:3` and so on when taken), the `NewMatch` that placed it and the streams it split. The first match of a level makes its stage; the others join it and split the level's stream. Each evaporator stream is matched to every hot stream whose cooler starts at least `approach` K above the evaporator's hot end, in a new stage after the network's last, hottest evaporator first; each condenser stream to every cold stream whose heater starts at least `approach` K below the condenser's cold end, in a new stage ahead of the first, so the hottest condenser is at stage 1. Temperatures are the first period's; a stream nothing can serve gets no match. A search with a `HeatPumpStep` starts from this network; each match's move keeps its placement and splits, and repipes its process stream only (the heat pump's stream is new).
- `apply_relocation(network, exchanger_id, move)` (`moves.py`): the network with that recovery exchanger moved to the match, keeping its id and installed area.
- `NewUtility`, `new_utility_candidates(network, *, hot_streams, cold_streams, hot_utilities, cold_utilities)`, `apply_new_utility(network, move, exchanger_id)` (`moves.py`): a new end-of-stream heater or cooler, the ones a network lacks, and the network with one added (no installed area).
- `new_splits(network, move)` (`moves.py`): the streams the match splits.
- `RetrofitSettings.approach` is the minimum approach (K) on every exchanger; `None` gives each exchanger the sum of its two sides' temperature contributions in the fixed-structure model and its check, and the screens (new matches, relocations, heat pump placement) one approach, twice `THERMAL_DT_CONT` [search.py:RetrofitSettings, _Evaluator.screen_approach].
- `match_potential(network, ends, move, approach=..., weights=None, period_ends=None)`, `match_potentials(network, period_ends, move, approach=...)`, `utility_relief(network, move, potential)` (`potential` one value or one for each period), `utility_duties(network, kind, stream)`, `joint_relief(cooling, heating, weights, cap=inf)`, `screen_new_matches(network, ends, approach=..., limit=..., processes=None, weights=None, period_ends=None)` (`moves.py`): the heat a new match could recover at its position in a solved network (and in each period of it), the utility it could cut at first order, a stream's cooler or heater duty in each period, the utility a link between a cooled and a heated stream could cut (the smaller side in each period, then the period-weighted mean), and the most promising new matches (with `processes`, stream to process zone, only pairs of one process; a stream it leaves out joins no pair).
- `stream_process_zones(zone)` (`processes.py`): each stream key of a zone (`Heating.H1`) mapped to the address (`Site/Heating`) of the outermost process zone at or below it that holds the stream, so same-named process zones under different parents stay apart; streams outside every process zone are left out.
- `stage_count(network)` (`moves.py`): the stage count, or the deepest recovery stage.
- `mark_dominated(designs)` (`frontier.py`): the designs with `dominated` set.
- `LOOP_SPAN`, `HeatRecoveryLoop`, `heat_recovery_loop_candidates(ends, processes, *, approach)`, `loop_placement(hot, cold, *, approach)`, `screen_heat_recovery_loops(network, candidates, *, limit)`, `loop_cooler_name`, `loop_heater_name`, `with_loop_utilities(arrays, loops)` (`loops.py`): a heat recovery loop from a hot stream of one process to a cold stream of another at one temperature (K), the candidates for every such pair, the screen that ranks them, the names of its cooler (a cold utility) and heater (a hot utility), `Loop <hot>-><cold>:cooler` and `:heater` with `\` and `>` in a stream name escaped by a backslash so no two pairs share a name, and the solver arrays with those utilities added.
- `with_constant_utilities(arrays, utilities)` (`loops.py`): the solver arrays with constant-temperature utilities (side, name, inlet, outlet, price) appended after the problem's own; the loops use it.
- `retrofit_heat_pumps(problem, network, *, args=None)`, `as_piped_profiles(problem, network, *, period=0, grid_periods=())`, `as_piped_period_profiles(problem, network)`, `as_piped_heat_pump(problem, network, *, args=None, load_fraction=None, cycle=None)` (`heat_pump_targets.py`): the HPR service's ideal and as-piped heat pump targets of an existing network, as a `RetrofitHeatPumpTargets`; the source and sink profiles of the network's coolers and heaters that the as-piped target serves, in the period of index `period` (the temperatures of the periods of index `grid_periods` joining the grid without duty), and in every period by period id on one grid; and the as-piped target as the service solved it (`HeatPumpTargetOutputs`, with the condenser and evaporator streams and the work), the same solve at the same prices, `None` when the heaters carry no load; `load_fraction` caps the load at that share of the heaters' (`HPR_LOAD_FRACTION`, on a copy of the configuration), and `cycle` ("carnot" or "vapour_compression") solves a cascade of that cycle (`HPR_TYPE`, on a copy) whatever the zone's, `None` taking the zone's. `as_piped_heat_pump` takes a solved network; with several periods it is one machine designed for every period's profiles (`compute_shared_profile_heat_pump_target`), its `period_outputs` holding each period's streams and work.
- Subdirectories: none.

## Invariants
- Every exchanger of the existing network needs a positive installed `area`, else `ValueError` naming those without one, and a unique id, else `ValueError` naming the repeated ones; every exchanger id in `enhancement_factors` and `enhancement_pressure_drops`, and every stream in `enhancement_pressure_drops` and `pressure_drop_allowances`, must be in the existing network, else `ValueError` naming the unknown ones; an existing network the "retrofit" goal cannot solve at its installed areas (widened by `area_tolerance`) raises `ValueError` [search.py:run_retrofit_search].
- Depth 0 is the existing network; its utility cost is the base for savings. With `strategy="stepwise"` each depth tries, from every design in the beam, added area on each existing unit at its cap (active and needing at least 99 % of its widened area; when the solve reports `area_values`, only units whose area value exceeds $0.001/y per m2, most valuable first) and the `new_match_candidates` best screened new matches; a structure (recovery matches by stage, and the enlarged units) is solved at most once per search. Children are ranked by net annual benefit, the best `beam_width` form the next beam, and the best is the depth's frontier design. The search stops at `max_modifications` or when a depth has no solved child [search.py:run_retrofit_search, _children, _signature].
- A new match's modification reports its stage in the design's network, after any stages that later modifications inserted; `new_stage` says whether it was added in a stage of its own [search.py:_Evaluator._modification].
- With `strategy="structure"` (the default) a depth adds one structural change (no area-addition children): the `ceiling_candidates` screened new matches and the relocations of each beam design, with area additions stripped, are deduplicated, ranked by the utility cost of their structure with no installed areas (the "utility cost" goal, so each utility's price decides which carries the load; the order, ties broken by generation order, does not depend on the thread count), and the `new_match_candidates` best of each kind of change (new match, relocation) are solved twice: with every existing unit enlargeable, then with only the units whose added area is at least 1 % of installed and 1 m2, each priced as an area addition. If the second solve fails the structure is solved without added area. With `enhancement_factors`, the kept units that have a factor are then enhanced and the structure sized again the same way (two more solves), and the sized design with the better net annual benefit is kept [search.py:run_retrofit_search, _structure_only, _Evaluator.best_ceilings, ceiling, sized, _sized_with].
- A design's network gives each existing unit the area it has after the retrofit, installed plus any added (the model's area for it), not the area its duty needs, or its slices' design area where that is larger (a unit rated within the area tolerance can need a little more than it has, and an exchanger's area may not be below its slices'), so the network validates when loaded again; verification and the recorded rating allowance are judged against that area [search.py:_with_owned_areas].
- A candidate whose solve does not converge, raises while its solution is read, or whose solved network fails verification is dropped and counted: `result.failed_solves[n - 1]` is the count for step `n` (ceiling solves included; a structure the model cannot build, and a move that does nothing, are not solves and not counted). The search warns (`UserWarning`) when more than a quarter of `evaluated_designs` failed [search.py:run_retrofit_search, _Evaluator._failed].
- The sizing solve does not see an area addition's fixed cost (`area_addition_cost`), so each depth's best design is solved again with each kept addition dropped in turn; the drop that raises net annual benefit most stands, and the pass repeats from there until no drop pays. Only the depth's best design is treated (the next depth sizes every kept design's area afresh), which costs one solve per kept addition and one more round per drop that stands [search.py:run_retrofit_search, _Evaluator.paying].
- Added area on an existing unit is at most `max_added_area_fraction` times its installed area when that is set (passed to the model spec); a negative, NaN or infinite fraction raises `ValueError` before any solve [search.py:run_retrofit_search, _Evaluator.evaluate].
- With `relocation_cost` set, each depth also tries relocations: an existing recovery unit not yet moved, to any screened match sharing one of its streams, scored by the match's potential less the unit's present duty (first period). The stepwise search tries the `relocation_candidates` best positive scores; the structure search passes up to `ceiling_relocations` moves (default 400), best score first whatever its sign, to the ceiling ranking; a unit that is the only exchanger on one of its streams is moved only to matches on that stream. A relocation adds no area; its fixed cost is `relocation_cost` plus `split_cost` per new split (splits counted without the moved unit). The search signature keys each recovery match by the existing unit installed there, so different relocations to one match set stay distinct [search.py:_children, _relocations, _signature, _Evaluator._modification].
- Relocating keeps the old stage, even if it empties [moves.py:apply_relocation].
- `utility_switch_candidates(network, *, units, hot_utilities, cold_utilities)` and `apply_utility_switch(network, exchanger_id, utility)` (`moves.py`): each heater or cooler among `units` paired with each other utility of its kind (heaters first), and the network with one served by another utility, keeping its id, process stream and installed area.
- With `utility_switch_cost` set, each depth also tries a utility switch for each existing heater or cooler not yet switched, to each other utility of its kind (the network's own utilities; ones the existing network does not name keep their solver names). It is a structural change with its own ceiling quota, never paired with a new end heater or cooler (`new_utility_units`), priced at `utility_switch_cost` with no area; any area the unit then gains is an area addition of its own. The modification reports the new utility and the stream as `hot_stream` and `cold_stream` [search.py:_switch_steps, _Evaluator._modification].
- A new utility candidate is a heater from each hot utility on each cold stream without an end heater, then a cooler from each cold utility on each hot stream without an end cooler; a utility exchanger after a stage does not count [moves.py:new_utility_candidates].
- With `new_utility_units`, each new match and relocation is also tried with each new utility candidate of its network, as one modification: one more `new_unit_cost` and the utility exchanger's area cost on top of the move's. The pair is rejected when the utility exchanger has no duty. New utility exchangers are named `new:<utility>-><stream>` (heater) or `new:<stream>-><utility>` (cooler). The search signature includes the utility exchangers, so a paired design is distinct from its unpaired twin [search.py:_children, _modification, _signature, _new_utility_id].
- With `enhancement_factors` (existing exchanger id to factor), the stepwise search also tries, from each design, enhancing each unit that has a factor and is not yet enhanced, at its cap or already given area (an insert may replace part of that area): the model multiplies its capacity by the factor (`enhanced_recovery`, `enhanced_utilities`). Its fixed cost is `enhancement_cost` plus `enhancement_area_cost` times its installed area, with no added area. With `enhancement_pressure_drops` (id to the stream the insert is on and the kPa it adds) and `pressure_drop_allowances` (stream to the kPa it can take), a set of enhanced units whose drops on a stream sum above its allowance, or one with an insert on a stream its exchanger no longer serves after a relocation, is never tried: the stepwise search skips such a child and the structure search enhances kept units, best estimated return per kPa first (the area addition an insert can stand in for, its fixed cost and the added area's cost, less the insert's cost, over its pressure drop; no pressure drop ranks first), only while they fit [search.py:_within_pressure_drop, _Evaluator._insert_return]; the signature includes the enhanced units, and the structure strategy strips enhancements with area additions [search.py:_children, _structure_only, _signature, _Evaluator.evaluate, _modification].
- A real-fluid heat pump whose machine capital cannot be priced (its cycle fails at the solved duty with `ValueError` or `RuntimeError`) is dropped as a modification, and the search goes on [search.py:_Evaluator._modification].
- Every solved design is checked with the duty optimiser's `verify_solution` (approaches, heat balances, and areas, each existing unit's installed area widened by `area_tolerance`, by the model's rating allowance for it (`rating_allowances`: how much more area exact or slice sizing needs than the model's calibrated Chen cap, at least 1; 1 for a model that reports none) and, for an enhanced unit, by its factor, as the model rates it) and rejected on any failure; a rejected existing network raises the `ValueError` above. An accepted design's network records, per existing unit, that widening (its rated area over its reported area) in `source_metadata["rating_area_allowances"]` and the largest in `summary_metrics["rating_area_allowance"]`, so `verify_network_feasibility` judges it as it was rated [search.py:_Evaluator.evaluate, _widened, _with_rating_allowances].
- A candidate whose structure the model cannot build (a `ValueError` from `FixedStructureModel`, such as streams with no utility exchanger whose loads cannot balance) is dropped, in the ceiling ranking and when solved; for the existing network the error propagates [search.py:_Evaluator.evaluate, ceiling].
- A candidate whose solve raises `ValueError` while the model reads its solution (a solved duty that runs past a segmented stream's profile) is dropped the same way, in the ceiling ranking and when solved; for the existing network the error propagates [search.py:_Evaluator.evaluate, ceiling].
- A stream with several segments has a zero placeholder flowrate in the arrays; the stream ends give it its segments' duty over its temperature span, per period [search.py:_stream_ends].
- A design is rejected when any of its modifications does nothing: a unit with no duty or, other than a relocation, less than 1e-3 m2 of added area [search.py:_Evaluator._modification].
- A modification's area cost is `COSTING_HX_AREA_COEFF * added ** COSTING_HX_AREA_EXP` in $; its fixed cost is `new_unit_cost` plus `split_cost` per new split for a new match, `area_addition_cost` for added area. A new match, relocation or paired new utility exchanger also costs `repiping_cost` per stream it repipes, named in `repiped_streams`: both streams of a new match; the streams a relocated unit leaves and joins, less one it stays on in the same stage (not a new stage); and a paired utility exchanger's stream, each stream once [search.py:_structural_steps, _relocation_repiping, _children, _Evaluator._modification].
- A relocated unit keeps its id, so it can take added area like any existing unit: in the structure search's sizing solves, and in the stepwise search when it is at its cap [search.py:_Evaluator.sized, _Evaluator.evaluate]. Capital is their sum, annualised with the capital recovery factor of `COSTING_DISCOUNT_RATE` and `COSTING_SERVICE_LIFE`; net annual benefit is savings less annualised capital and payback is capital over savings (`None` without savings) [search.py:_Evaluator.evaluate, _modification].
- The stream ends the screens read (supply and target temperature, heat capacity flowrate) cover every period: the flowrate is the period-weighted mean, so a stream that is off part of the time counts for the share it runs, and the temperatures are the weighted mean over the periods the stream runs. The new-match and loop screens and the relocation scoring read a solved network's duties and temperatures as period-weighted means (`period_mean`, with the weights of `arrays["period_weights"]`; called without weights they read the first period), so statements below that a screen reads "the first period" hold only for such a call. Utility relief pairs the two streams' duties period by period before the mean (`joint_relief`): cooling in one period and heating in another relieve nothing. The new-match screen also gets each period's own stream ends (`_period_stream_ends`, a stream that is off having a flowrate of 0): a match's potential is then the period-weighted mean of each period's potential (`match_potentials`: that period's smaller flowrate and temperatures, a period's temperature read from the first exchanger at or after the position that has a reading there), and its relief is capped by each period's potential, so a pair that never runs together has no potential and is not proposed. Relocation scoring still reads a match's potential from the period means [search.py:_stream_ends, _period_stream_ends, _stream_rows, _Evaluator.__init__, _structural_steps, _relocations] [moves.py:period_mean, match_potential, match_potentials, utility_relief, utility_duties, joint_relief] [loops.py:screen_heat_recovery_loops].
- Moves use the stream names of the existing network: a solver stream name maps to the existing name it ends with (`H1` for `Process A.H1`) when exactly one matches and no solver stream has that name exactly; each name the existing network uses is checked against all solver streams, so a bare name that fits streams of several zones (`H1` for `A.H1` and `B.H1`) raises `ValueError` naming them, even when another exchanger names one of them in full. New exchangers are named `new:<hot>-><cold>`, with `:2`, `:3` and so on when taken [search.py:_existing_names, _new_id].
- When the existing network's streams span several process zones (by `stream_process_zones` of the problem's master zone, under the existing network's stream names), new matches and relocations are screened only between streams of one process (a stream outside every process zone joins none), and `run_retrofit_search` raises one `UserWarning` naming the zones and saying the site utility balance is not modelled (utilities are priced unit by unit), and, with `heat_recovery_loops` on, that processes are linked only through a new heat recovery loop. Heat pumps are not restricted. A network within one process screens every pair [search.py:run_retrofit_search, _spanned_processes, _structural_steps, _relocations] [moves.py:screen_new_matches] [processes.py:stream_process_zones].
- With `heat_recovery_loops` (default on; `HENS_RETROFIT_LOOPS` through `design.retrofit`) and an existing network spanning several process zones, each depth also tries heat recovery loops, the only link between processes: a candidate for each hot stream and cold stream of different processes (both in a process zone) that a loop can carry heat between, at `loop_placement`'s temperature and with the heat it can carry there as the candidate's `capacity`. Where the targets are at least `2 * screen approach + LOOP_SPAN` (1 K) apart that is midway between them, so each stream reaches its target at least the approach from the loop's nearer end (the loop runs over `LOOP_SPAN` about its temperature) and the loop can take all of the hot stream's end cooling. Otherwise the loop serves part of each stream, the hot stream above it and the cold stream below it, and runs where the smaller of the heat it can collect (`CP_hot * (supply - max(target, T - LOOP_SPAN / 2 + approach))`: the hot stream leaves the counter-current loop cooler the approach above the loop's cool end) and deliver (`CP_cold * (min(target, T + LOOP_SPAN / 2 - approach) - supply)`: the cold stream leaves the loop heater the approach below its warm end) is largest, within `supply_cold + approach + LOOP_SPAN / 2 <= T <= supply_hot - approach - LOOP_SPAN / 2` (each stream enters against the loop's other end); a pair with none gets no loop. Both are read from the stream ends, not a solved network, so the solve may find less [loops.py:heat_recovery_loop_candidates, loop_placement]. A candidate whose cooler or heater name is already a solver stream or utility name, or the unprefixed tail of one, is left out. Each loop's heater (warm end to cool end) and cooler (the other way) are added to the solver arrays as free constant-temperature utilities after the heat pumps', with the first utility's film coefficient, area costs and temperature contributions [loops.py:with_loop_utilities] [search.py:_Evaluator.__init__].
- The `new_match_candidates` loops that could relieve the most utility (the hot stream's cooler duty capped by the cold stream's heater duty and by the loop's `capacity` in each period, then the period-weighted mean, `joint_relief`; ties by names) and are not yet installed are tried from each design (the structure search ranks `ceiling_candidates` of them, with their own quota): the loop cooler at the end of the hot stream and the loop heater at the end of the cold stream, as one `heat_recovery_loop` modification, never paired with a new utility exchanger. Both solves link the two duties as equal in every period (`FixedStructureSpec.loops`; no heat loss is modelled). A loop costs two `new_unit_cost`, its two exchangers' added area by the area law, `heat_recovery_loop_cost` and `repiping_cost` for its two streams; it reports `loop_temperature` (degC) and `loop_duty` (its heater's largest duty), and its duties (period-weighted) are left out of the design's hot and cold utility [search.py:_loop_steps, _loop_links, _Evaluator._modification, _Evaluator.evaluate] [loops.py:screen_heat_recovery_loops].
- A frontier design is dominated when one from an earlier step has at least its net annual benefit; steps, not modification records, count, as a structural step can keep several area additions. `selected` breaks a net-benefit tie by the fewest steps [frontier.py:mark_dominated] [../../../contracts/synthesis/retrofit.py:HeatExchangerNetworkRetrofitResult.selected].
- Inserting a stage at `s` moves every exchanger (recovery, and utility placed after a stage) with a stage of `s` or more one stage later and adds one to the network's stage count; end utilities (no stage) stay at the stream ends [moves.py:apply_new_match].
- A match joining a stage splits each of its streams that already has a recovery match in that stage; a match in a new stage splits nothing [moves.py:new_splits].
- A hot stream's temperature entering a stage is the source inlet of its first exchanger at or after that point (recovery in that stage or later, coolers after that stage or at the end), else its target; a cold stream's likewise in its own direction (last stage to first). Readings are from the network's first period [moves.py:_hot_temperature, _cold_temperature].
- A match's potential is the smaller heat capacity flowrate times the hot less cold temperature at its position less the approach, at least zero. The screen tries every hot-cold pair joining each stage (except a stage the pair already shares) and in a new stage at each of the `S + 1` positions, keeps positive potentials, and ranks them by utility relief (the potential capped by the hot stream's total cooler duty and the cold stream's total heater duty in each period, then the period-weighted mean, `joint_relief`), then potential, fewest new splits, stage, joining before a new stage, then names [moves.py:match_potential, utility_relief, screen_new_matches].

- A `heat_pump_cycle` in the settings is refused with `ValueError` before any solve: the search's linked heat pump move (an evaporator and a condenser at two stream ends, linked by a COP) was retired on 2026-11-24. The cycle and `heat_pump_cost` are the stream step's (`application/heat_pump_retrofit.py`); a search starts from a heat pump only as streams (`heat_pump=`) [search.py:run_retrofit_search].
- `retrofit_heat_pumps` runs `compute_profile_heat_pump_target` (the HPR service) twice with the master zone's configuration: the ideal target on the direct-integration problem table's `H(net-hot)` and `H(net-cold)` columns (the targets must be cached), the as-piped target on `as_piped_profiles` (each segment shifted by its process stream's temperature contribution and multiplier). A period-valued HPR load is read at the problem's first period (lowest index). A network whose heaters or coolers lack solved process-side temperatures in their first period is first solved by `run_retrofit_search` at depth 0 (default settings) and its existing design used [heat_pump_targets.py:retrofit_heat_pumps, _solved].
- Both targets are searched at the prices they are reported at: the configuration given to the HPR service is a copy with `COSTING_HPR_PRICE_RATIO_HEAT_TO_ELE` and `COSTING_HPR_PRICE_RATIO_COOLING_WATER_TO_ELE` set to the network's hot and cold utility prices over `COSTING_HPR_ELE_PRICE`, and `COSTING_HPR_REFRIGERATION_ETA_II` set to 0 so that cooling left below the cooling water level is priced at the cold utility too, not from the default refrigeration machine's COP; the zone's own configuration is not changed, and with no electricity price the ratios stay as configured [heat_pump_targets.py:_at_network_prices].
- `as_piped_profiles` reads one period (the first by default), skipping an exchanger that is inactive there or lacks its process-side temperatures (off, with no duty): each cooler becomes a hot segment and each heater a cold one from the process stream's solved inlet to outlet temperature (K, converted to degC) at the exchanger's duty, with the process stream's temperature contribution (the stream named as is or as the tail of a zone-qualified name; none, or a tail several zones share, raises `ValueError` naming it). Sources and sinks are cascaded separately on the grid of all segments, so the profiles hold no recovery between a cooler and a heater [heat_pump_targets.py:as_piped_profiles, _process_stream].
- `as_piped_period_profiles` gives every period's profiles on the grid of every period's segments, so a shared heat pump design compares the periods interval by interval. With several periods `as_piped_heat_pump` passes them to `compute_shared_profile_heat_pump_target` at the first period's prices, each period weighted by the master zone's period weights; a period whose heaters carry no load runs no machine and keeps its weight, and with no load in any period the result is `None` [heat_pump_targets.py:as_piped_period_profiles, as_piped_heat_pump].
- Each target's starting hot and cold utility is the largest magnitude of its sink and source profile; it relieves the condenser heat from hot utility and the evaporator heat less the service's ambient air source (`Q_amb_hot`) from cold utility (the ambient sink, `Q_amb_cold`, is source heat left to air and relieves nothing). With no load the service returns `None` and the target delivers nothing. Prices ($/kW/y) are each side's utilities the network's heaters (or coolers) use, weighted by their first-period duties (the side's cheapest utility when none is in use), and `COSTING_HPR_ELE_PRICE` times the annual cost scale; both targets take the first period; no heat pump capital is counted [heat_pump_targets.py:_summary, _utility_price, retrofit_heat_pumps].

## Side Effects
- `run_retrofit_search` runs direct heat integration through `problem.target` when no targets are cached, and solves each candidate with the configured `HENS_SOLVER_EVM` solver (an external executable via Pyomo), in-process; a depth's ceiling solves, then its sizing (structure) or candidate (stepwise) solves, run on `HENS_MAX_PARALLEL` threads when it is above 1 (one on Pyodide), keeping generation order, with a lock on the solve counter [search.py:_Evaluator.map]. It removes each candidate model's run directory once its values are read, solved or not [search.py:run_retrofit_search, _Evaluator.evaluate].
- `moves.py`, `frontier.py`, `loops.py` and `processes.py` are pure.
- `retrofit_heat_pumps` runs the HPR service's multistart search twice (CoolProp cycles for vapour compression), in-process, and a depth-0 retrofit search (the solver) for an unsolved network, with `approach=None` (each exchanger held to its two streams' contributions) as `design.retrofit` rates its existing network by default.
